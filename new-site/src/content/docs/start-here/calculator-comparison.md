# We reviewed top 20 novated lease calculators. Only one shows you true savings.

We Googled ["novated lease calculator"](https://www.google.com/search?q=novated+lease+calculator), took the first 20 results, and stress-tested each one: what does it actually show, how does it derive the savings figure, and can you trust the number it gives you?

While many calculators compete by slickness and user-friendliness, the main aim of this review is to assess whether they help users **make a genuinely informed financial decision** — or whether they simply produce a large "you save $XX,XXX" figure and nudge you toward a sales enquiry.

:::warning[What we found]
- **All of the top 20** calculators returned by Google are run by companies that sell novated leases.
- **18 of 20** show only a "tax saving" — a figure that can overstate your true benefit by tens of thousands of dollars. One further site shows both, but buries the net saving in an expandable section.
- **5 of 20** won't show you a single number until you hand over your phone number and email to a salesperson.
- **None of the 20** compare a novated lease against the most realistic alternative: keeping the car you already have.
- **Two independent calculators provide honest outputs** — and neither appears in the first two pages of search results. They are included here for completeness.
:::

## Criteria explained

| Criterion | What is being assessed |
|---|---|
| **Affiliation** | Whether the calculator is hosted by a commercial provider. Affiliated calculators have a financial incentive to show favourable results. |
| **Benchmark shown** | Whether the headline "saving" is a *tax saving* (income tax, Medicare levy & GST savings only — [overstates the benefit](/costs-and-savings/false-saving-example/)) or a *net saving* (after all costs vs a realistic alternative — the figure that truly matters). |
| **vs Cash / vs Loan / vs Keep car** | Whether the calculator compares the novated lease against a cash purchase, a car loan, or keeping the current car. A novated lease is only worth taking if it beats a realistic alternative. |
| **Car selection** | Whether a specific car model can be searched to pre-fill parameters, or whether everything must be entered manually. |
| **Interest presented** | Whether the lease effective interest rate is visible +/- adjustable. |
| **Residual shown** | Whether the balloon payment at end of lease is mentioned as part of the true cost. |
| **Assumptions disclosed** | Whether the calculator states its key assumptions and methodology clearly. |
| **Caveats mentioned** | Whether [caveats and side effects of novated leasing](/start-here/is-it-worth-it/#start-with-a-holistic-view-rather-than-the-savings-figure) are noted (e.g. RFBA impact on HECS/HELP, childcare subsidy, Medicare levy surcharge, reduced borrowing capacity, early-termination risk, potentially reduced superannuation contribution). |

## Summary findings

### Commercial affiliation

All 20 calculators returned by Google are affiliated with novated lease providers. Neither independent calculator appears in organic search results without specifically searching for it by name.

### Net saving vs tax saving

Of the 22 calculators reviewed:

- **3** (Toyota Fleet Management, Leasecheck.au and this site) correctly benchmark against a genuine net saving
- **1** (Leaselab) shows a tax saving headline with a net saving tucked in an expandable section
- **18** show only a tax saving — or a "saving" defined as the cost of the lease paid post-tax without a GST exemption, which is not a realistic alternative. 

The tax saving figure [consistently overstates the true benefit](/costs-and-savings/false-saving-example/), such that it's entirely possible for a claimed $21,000 tax savings to translate to $5,000 loss when compared to a cash purchase alternative. 

### Comparison against alternatives

A novated lease is only worth taking if it is genuinely cheaper than the alternative. Of the 22:

- **4** compare against a cash purchase (Toyota, Leaselab, Leasecheck.au and this site)
- **4** compare against a car loan (Toyota, Leaselab, Leasecheck.au and this site)
- **1** compare against keeping the current car (this site)

No other calculator compares against keeping the current car.

### Calculators used as lead generation funnels

Five calculators (AANT, Driva, Flare HR, Salary Packaging Australia, StreetFleet) require you to submit your email address and/or phone number before displaying any result. You are handing over your contact details to a sales team before you have seen a single number.

Of the remaining calculators, most display a headline "you save $X" figure with no explanation of how it was derived. Only a handful disclose the benchmark used, and fewer still explain what is and is not included in the running costs budget.

### What most calculators get wrong

The patterns that appear across virtually every affiliated calculator:

- **Tax saving presented as the headline saving** — framing income tax reduction as "the saving" [which is fallacious](/costs-and-savings/false-saving-example/). 
- **Implicit benchmark seldom explained** — most calculators compare against a hypothetical "same lease paid with post-tax dollars, no GST saving", which is not a realistic counterfactual. 
- **Interest rate hidden or absent** — only a small minority show the lease interest rate, making it impossible to assess whether the underlying financing is competitive
- **Residual value omitted** — the balloon payment at end of lease is a real future liability; most calculators do not include it in the cost calculation. Without the residual in view, a lease can appear cheaper than a car loan on a fortnightly basis — while leaving a large liability unpaid at the end.
- **Caveats absent** — fewer than half mention any [caveats of novated leasing](/start-here/is-it-worth-it/) (e.g. RFBA impact on HECS/HELP, childcare subsidy, Medicare levy surcharge, reduced borrowing capacity, early-termination risk, reduced superannuation contributions). Even those that do typically mention only RFBA and nothing else.
