# Comparison of novated lease calculators

There are many novated lease calculators available online. This page systematically reviews the first twenty calculators returned by a Google search for "novated lease calculator", assessing them across a range of criteria relevant to a person trying to understand whether a novated lease is genuinely worth it.

The goal is not to rank calculators by slickness or features, but to assess whether they help users **make a genuinely informed financial decision** — or whether they simply produce a large "you save $X" figure and nudge you toward a sales enquiry.

---

## Why this comparison matters

Most novated lease calculators are produced by companies that profit from you taking out a lease. This creates an inherent conflict of interest: the calculator's job is not to inform you, but to convert you.

A well-designed calculator should:

- be transparent about its assumptions,
- compare the novated lease against a realistic alternative (cash, loan, or keeping your current car),
- show a **net saving**, not just a tax saving,
- disclose whether it is affiliated with a commercial provider, and
- allow you to understand *why* the number is what it is, not just *what* the number is.

The criteria below reflect these expectations.

---

## Criteria explained

### Commercial affiliation

Whether the calculator is hosted by or affiliated with a commercial novated lease provider, salary packaging company, or car dealership. A commercially affiliated calculator has a financial incentive to show favourable results.

Rated as:

- **Independent** — no commercial affiliation apparent,
- **Affiliated** — produced by or directly linked to a provider, or
- **Unclear** — affiliation not stated.

---

### Benchmark of saving

What the calculator presents as the headline "saving". Two very different figures are commonly used:

- **Tax saving** — the reduction in income tax alone, which overstates the true benefit by ignoring the cost of the lease itself,
- **Net saving** — the genuine after-all-costs saving versus an alternative, which is the figure that actually matters.

A calculator showing only a tax saving without contextualising it against the total cost of the lease is misleading by omission.

---

### Comparison against alternatives

A novated lease is only worth taking if it is cheaper than the realistic alternative. The most useful comparisons are:

- **Cash purchase** — the most common benchmark,
- **Car loan** — relevant for those who would otherwise finance a car,
- **Keep current car** — the most conservative but often most realistic alternative.

---

### Car selection / ease of estimate

Whether the calculator allows you to select or search for a specific car model to pre-fill relevant parameters (purchase price, running costs, etc.), or whether you are required to manually enter everything from scratch.

---

### Other criteria

| Criterion | What is being assessed |
|---|---|
| Inputs required | How many fields must be filled before a result is shown (proxy for friction and data collection intent) |
| Sensible defaults | Whether the calculator pre-fills realistic default values |
| Editable interest rate | Whether the lease interest rate is visible and adjustable |
| FBT method shown | Whether the FBT calculation method (statutory vs operating cost) is disclosed |
| Pre-tax vs post-tax split | Whether the breakdown between pre-tax and post-tax contributions is shown |
| GST saving itemised | Whether the upfront GST saving on the vehicle is shown separately |
| Running costs included | Whether fuel, rego, insurance, tyres, and servicing are included — and whether this is clearly disclosed |
| Residual value shown | Whether the residual / balloon payment is included in the cost calculation |
| Admin fees included | Whether salary packaging administration fees are factored in |
| Assumptions disclosed | Whether the calculator states its key assumptions clearly |
| ATO rates cited | Whether the calculator references the ATO tax year or rates it is using |
| Methodology explained | Whether an accompanying article or explanation describes how the calculation works |
| Account required | Whether creating an account or submitting contact details is required to see results |
| Mobile friendly | Whether the calculator is usable on a phone |
| Last updated | Whether a "last updated" date is shown |
